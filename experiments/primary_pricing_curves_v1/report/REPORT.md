# Primary pricing curves: C vs S and C vs remaining maturity tau

Generated by `scripts/make_primary_figures.py` on the frozen controlled two-timescale benchmark.
Every price comes from a pricing equation through an already-validated pricer. No curve is offset,
rescaled, smoothed or reordered anywhere in this experiment. The maturity ordering is **checked**,
never imposed.

## Setting

| item | value |
|---|---|
| benchmark | controlled, `r = q = 0`, so `F = S` |
| strike | `K = 100` (one common strike) |
| S grid | 601 points, `0.60K <= S <= 1.40K`, identical for every model |
| maturities | 7, 30, 90, 180, 365, 730 days (predeclared) |
| tau grid (C vs tau) | 601 points, 7 to 730 days |
| variance state | frozen `FIXED_TOTAL_TWIST`: fast-heavy `v_f=0.035, v_s=0.005`; slow-heavy `v_f=0.005, v_s=0.035`; both total 0.04 -> 20% instantaneous |
| mean reversion | `kappa_f = 10.7526`, `kappa_s = 0.9491` (ratio 11.3x), frozen |

Models, all with hard-coded / frozen parameters:

* **Black-Scholes, constant sigma = 20%** - analytic formula, matches the instantaneous volatility of the state.
* **Black-Scholes, calibrated term sigma(tau)** - the frozen non-leaking `BS_TERM` fit. Kept so Black-Scholes is not weakened.
* **Single Heston (exact)** - frozen strongest fit (global search + 12 restarts) for the same state.
* **Double Heston (exact)** - frozen published two-factor parameters.
* **Double-Heston PINN** - `dh_pinn_v5` FINAL, two-seed mean. Drawn only inside its trained domain `|log(S/K)| <= 0.36`.
* **Black-Scholes PINN** - branch `black-scholes-with-pinn`. It owns `r = 0.03`, `q = 0.01`, calibrated `sigma = 0.19999906`, so it is shown **only against its own analytic target**, never on the `r = q = 0` benchmark.

## Financial validation

58 model x maturity curves, finite-difference Delta and Gamma, plus the European bounds.

| check | result |
|---|---|
| `dC/dS >= 0` | **0 violations** out of 58 curves |
| `C >= max(S-K,0)` | **0 violations**; worst time value `-4.6e-12` (floating point) |
| `C <= S` | **0 violations** |
| `d2C/dS2 >= 0` | 0 violations for every exact pricer and for the DH-PINN (worst `-1e-9`, floating point). 279 points belong to the **BS-PINN** only |
| BS-PINN gamma | worst negative gamma is `-0.43%` of that curve's peak gamma; **0 points** breach a 1%-of-peak threshold |

The gamma wiggle is confined to the Black-Scholes **neural surrogate** at 7 and 30 days, is smaller
than half a percent of peak gamma, and is a property of the network, not of any pricing equation.
There are no economically meaningful violations.

**Maturity ordering (verified, not imposed).** All 30 adjacent-maturity comparisons are increasing in
tau. Smallest gaps: `BS_TERM 1.15e-2`, `PINN 1.15e-2`, `BS20 1.3e-19`, and `DH -7.2e-14`, `SH -2.8e-14`
(both at floating-point level). The neon-reference ordering `C(730d) > C(365d) > ... > C(7d)` therefore
comes out of the equations.

**tau -> 0 convergence to the payoff** (`data/tau_to_zero_convergence.csv`):

| tau | max abs(C - payoff), DH | mean abs(C - payoff), DH |
|---|---|---|
| 7 d | 1.1017 | 4.80e-2 |
| 1 d | 0.4174 | 6.86e-3 |
| 0.05 d | 0.0934 | 3.60e-4 |

At `tau = 0.01` day the characteristic-function quadrature declares itself unreliable for both Heston
pricers. That is recorded rather than hidden: it is a limit of the Fourier integration at sub-hour
maturities, not a model defect. Black-Scholes, which has a closed form, continues to `0.0418` there.

**Black-Scholes reference verified against the equation.** The analytic curves were checked at 30
points against an independent numerical integration of the risk-neutral expectation:
max absolute difference `6.1e-08`. The curves are computed from the formula, never inferred from
implied-volatility data.

## Numerical differences against exact Double Heston (price units, K = 100)

RMSE over the shared S grid:

| tau | BS const 20% | BS term sigma(tau) | Single Heston | DH-PINN |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 d | 0.0424 | 0.0428 | 0.0398 | **0.00027** |
| 90 d | 0.1031 | 0.1056 | 0.0659 | **0.00041** |
| 365 d | 0.3815 | 0.1613 | 0.0279 | **0.00096** |
| 730 d | 0.9747 | 0.1178 | 0.0960 | **0.00136** |

The DH-PINN reproduces the exact Double-Heston price to roughly 1e-3 of a currency unit, two to three
orders of magnitude below the gap to either baseline. The raw C(S) curves sit almost on top of each
other, which is expected and is not manipulated away; the residual panels are where the differences live.

**The economically important figure is 7.** Fast-heavy and slow-heavy carry the *same* 20%
instantaneous volatility, so Black-Scholes and any one-factor fit calibrated to that instantaneous
level cannot tell them apart. The exact two-factor price differs by up to **1.45** currency units at
730 days near the money. That spread is the structural content a second variance factor buys.

# Normalised companion figures (`figures_normalised/`)

Generated by `scripts/make_normalised_figures.py`. Each panel divides by a **stated quantity applied
identically to every model**. No curve is shifted or offset vertically, and the un-normalised
primaries in `figures/01..10` are unchanged and remain the reference.

A normalisation was kept only if it was **measured** to separate the models. Two were tested and
rejected; the numbers are in `data/rejected_normalisations.csv`.

| # | file | normaliser |
|---|---|---|
| N1 | `N1_relative_price_difference.png` | divided by the exact DH price, in per cent |
| N2 | `N2_vega_normalised_residual.png` | residual divided by DH vega, in volatility points |
| N3 | `N3_sqrt_tau_normalised_ATM.png` | divided by `sqrt(tau)`, at the money |
| N4 | `N4_fast_vs_slow_normalised.png` | fast-heavy over slow-heavy, as a ratio and in vol points |

## Rejected normalisations, and why

**`C/S` against standardised moneyness `z = log(S/K)/(sigma sqrt(tau))`.** The intuition that
constant-sigma Black-Scholes collapses onto a single universal curve is **false**: `C/S` depends on
`z` *and* on `sigma sqrt(tau)` separately, through `K/S = exp(-z sigma sqrt(tau))`. Measured maturity
spread was **0.265 for Black-Scholes against 0.272 for Double Heston** - no contrast, so the figure
would have carried no information.

**`C/(S sigma sqrt(tau))` against `z`.** Measured spread **13.7% for Black-Scholes against 11.1% for
Single Heston**: Black-Scholes spreads *more*. Rejected.

**`C/sqrt(tau)` away from the money.** At `S/K = 0.90` the variation is 141.6% (BS), 149.7% (SH),
153.1% (DH); at `S/K = 1.10` it is 357.0%, 340.7%, 340.2%. No contrast, so N3 is used **at the money
only**, where it does discriminate.

## What the kept normalisations show

**N3 is the clean separation figure.** Dividing the at-the-money price by `sqrt(tau)` removes the
leading maturity scaling. Measured variation over 7-730 days:

| model | variation of `C/sqrt(tau)` |
|---|---|
| Black-Scholes, constant sigma = 20% | **0.33%** |
| Black-Scholes, calibrated sigma(tau) | 10.71% |
| Single Heston | 13.56% |
| Double Heston (exact) | 10.39% |
| Double-Heston PINN | 10.45% |

Constant-sigma Black-Scholes is a flat line to within a third of a per cent, because it has no
variance term structure to express. Everything with stochastic variance rises by roughly 10-14%.
The curves are fully separated and nothing was offset to achieve it.

**N2 expresses every residual in volatility points**, which is bounded and interpretable across the
whole surface. It is restricted to points where DH vega is at least 1% of its peak: vega goes to zero
in the deep wings, where the ratio diverges and carries no information. Each panel carries its own
y axis. At 90 days the spread runs from about +6 vol points deep out-of-the-money to -2.5 vol points
in-the-money for both Black-Scholes baselines, while the DH-PINN sits on zero.

**N1** is the same comparison as a percentage of the exact DH price, restricted to where `C_DH`
exceeds 1% of the at-the-money DH price for the same reason.
